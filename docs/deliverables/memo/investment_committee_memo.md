### Recommendation: **ACCEPT the mandate as proposed**, with the risk limits unchanged and spending set at about 2.65% a year
*Verdict confirmed by the team on 2026-10-30 (D34), in the brief's wording (D14).*

**What we propose.** Invest the $10M in seven exchange-traded funds and rebalance back to these weights every quarter:

| Bonds (AGG) | US stocks (SPY) | Technology (XLK) | International stocks (EFA) | Energy (XLE) | Gold (GLD) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 61% · $6.1M | 18% · $1.8M | 10% · $1.0M | 7% · $0.7M | 3% · $0.3M | 0.4% |

**Why this portfolio.**
- **It is the highest-return mix that respects every limit** the committee's principles imply:
  - a fall of no more than 25% from peak, even in a 2008-style crash;
  - a chance of less than 5% of such a fall within any 5 years;
  - no single fund carrying more than 40% of the risk on the worst days.
- **It earns more than the committee's own policy benchmark on a risk-adjusted basis.** Rebuilt every three years since 2008 using only the data available at the time, it earned 7.3% a year (the same as the benchmark) with a smaller worst fall (−16% vs −19%).
- **It is built from market-based expectations, not past returns.** The expected return is 5.8% a year, about 3.4% above expected inflation.

**What it means for spending.**
- **The brief sets no spending rate.** A common 4% payout would need about 7.2% a year, and reaching that requires accepting falls of 35–47%. That contradicts the committee's view that "avoiding large losses is more important than maximizing gains".
- **We recommend spending about 2.65% of the fund (about $265,000 next year).** This is the rate the portfolio can sustain while keeping its purchasing power and growing slightly.
- **Use the smoothed rule:** 70% of last year's spending (adjusted for inflation) plus 30% of 2.65% of the current fund value, so spending moves gradually.
- **If the university needs more,** it must accept more risk or use other income.

**What can go wrong, and how bad it could be.**
- **A repeat of 2008:** a fall of about 23% ($2.3M), recovered in about 3 years on average.
- **An energy-driven inflation shock** like a renewed Hormuz closure (oil +50%, interest rates up, stocks −30%): about 19% ($1.9M). In this case bonds fall too.
- **On a typical bad day:** 1 day in 100 the fund can lose 1.4% ($139,000) or more; on those days the average loss is 2.2% ($220,000).
- **Over 10 years:** about a 1-in-5 chance that the fund's real value ends below 90% of today's, and about a 1-in-4 chance that real spending is cut by more than 10% at some point.

**How confident we are.**
- **The daily risk model was chosen using 2015–2019 data only, then tested on 2020–2026.** It passed every standard statistical test, with 20 breaches against 17 expected.
- **Every limit also held on data the portfolio never saw.**
- **Limits:** history contains only one 2008-type crash, and the model cannot foresee a crisis unlike any in the data. Hence the monitoring rules below.

**What we ask the committee to approve.**
1. **The portfolio above,** rebalanced quarterly, with no tactical trading.
2. **A spending policy of about 2.65%** of fund value, applied through the smoothed rule.
3. **The monitoring ladder.** These are review points, not forced sales:

   | Fall from peak | Step | Action |
   |---|---|---|
   | 10% | watch | report to the committee |
   | 15% | review | review the risk model, stress tests and spending |
   | 20% | act | formal decision on risk and spending |
   | 25% | limit | mandatory decision: accept, adjust the risk limit, or reject |

4. **An annual review** of assumptions, weights and spending.
